# Abstract.
Unscheduled maintenance of turbofan engines represents one of the main drivers of cost and inoperability in commercial aviation. Predicting the **Remaining Useful Life** (**RUL**) through *Machine and Deep Learning* techniques enables failure anticipation with sufficient lead time to plan the intervention, supporting predictive maintenance strategies that optimise fleet availability and reduce corrective actions. This work develops and compares four machine learning models applied to RUL estimation in turbofan engines: *Random Forest* as a classical baseline model, and the *Deep Learning* architectures LSTM, Bi-LSTM and CNN-LSTM. The NASA N-CMAPSS 2 dataset (2021), developed by Arias Chao in collaboration with Kulkarni, Goebel and Fink, is used as the benchmark. Unlike its predecessor, it incorporates real flight conditions recorded on board commercial aircraft and relates the degradation process to the operational history of each engine. Following a pipeline of preprocessing, feature selection and sensor signal normalisation, each model is trained and evaluated using the field-standard metrics: RMSE and the asymmetric scoring function defined by NASA. The results obtained enable a systematic comparison between the classical ensemble approach and the recurrent and convolutional architectures, identifying the strengths and limitations of each under the operational variability conditions characteristic of the dataset.
